\begin{abstract}
This report records one reproducible observed-data research run on scikit-learn's
Wisconsin Breast Cancer dataset. It evaluates five classifiers using 455 training
and 114 held-out records. The positive target is explicitly \textbf{malignant},
not benign. All five models produced held-out AUROC between 0.9864 and 0.9879,
but this small benchmark cannot establish clinical validity. Reported measurements
come from the October 11, 2026 GitHub Actions run, not from an unverified
historical table or a prospective patient study.
\end{abstract}

\section{Source and target definition}
The genuine Wisconsin Breast Cancer dataset has 569 observations, 30 numerical
features, 212 malignant cases and 357 benign cases. In the original scikit-learn
labels 0 denotes malignant and 1 denotes benign. The current loader \emph{recodes}
the target so \textbf{1 denotes malignant} and 0 benign. The original measured
features are retained unchanged. The stratified 80/20 split with random state
123 contains 455 training and 114 test observations, of which 42 test cases
are malignant. These are historical diagnostic observations, not live clinical
records or a hospital API.

\section{Executed methodology}
This is the \texttt{configs/ci\_demo.yaml} benchmark, with seed 42,
two-fold stratified cross-validation on the training partition,
10-tree random forests (maximum depth 3), a 10-round XGBoost model
(maximum depth 6), logistic regression, and isotonic/Platt-calibrated
random forests (each with 3-fold internal calibration). Fitted models
are evaluated on the 114 untouched held-out rows using class-1 AUROC,
average precision (AUPRC), and Brier score. AUROC confidence intervals
use 30 percentile-bootstrap resamples. This limited CI configuration
\textbf{is not} the separate five-fold / 300-tree / 1000-bootstrap
research configuration, whose results have not been verified here.

The model used for SHAP explanation is selected solely by its training
cross-validation AUROC; the held-out outcomes are not used to choose
the explained model. Logistic regression achieved the largest observed
training CV AUROC (0.9964) and was selected for SHAP. No clinical
threshold or decision rule was validated.

The original uncalibrated random forest achieved Brier 0.0425, while
isotonic and Platt calibration achieved 0.0417 and 0.0404 respectively.
Both were worse than logistic regression's Brier 0.0289 in this run.
XGBoost achieved Brier 0.0683. AUROC differences across the models are
small relative to the reported bootstrap uncertainty. These results
do not support a claim that calibration universally improves clinical
outcomes or that any method is deployable for patient care.

\section{Reproduction and limitations}
The actual complete train/evaluate/plot/LaTeX run, all twelve passing
tests, and archived output are available at
\url{https://github.com/jibrankazi/Healthcare-ML/actions/runs/38164590942}.
The uploaded workflow artifact contains contemporaneous ROC, PR,
calibration and SHAP figures. Previously committed \texttt{paper/figures}
and \texttt{paper/main.pdf} are historical artifacts and have
\textbf{not} been regenerated for this corrected target definition;
they must not be cited as current evidence.

These data are small and curated. There is no external validation,
temporal validation, prospective study, independent calibration cohort,
hospital integration, or regulatory assessment. Probability quality,
operational usefulness and generalization to real patients remain
unproven. This is a reproducible research demonstration only.
